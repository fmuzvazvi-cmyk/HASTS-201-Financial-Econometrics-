definition = r"""
### Multicollinearity

It is quantified by the *Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)* for predictor \( j \):

$$
\text{VIF}_j = \frac{1}{1 - R_j^2}
$$

where \( R_j^2 \) is the coefficient of determination from regressing predictor \( j \) against all other predictors. A VIF > 5–10 indicates problematic multicollinearity.

*Description:* Multicollinearity occurs when two or more explanatory variables are highly linearly correlated, causing unstable coefficient estimates and inflated standard errors..
"""

Technical Definition: Skewness¶

Skewness measures the asymmetry of a distribution. The sample skewness is defined as:

$$ \text{Skewness} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \frac{x_i - \bar{x}}{s} \right)^3 $$

where ( \bar{x} ) is the sample mean, ( s ) is the sample standard deviation, and ( n ) is the number of observations.

  • Negative skewness (left‑skewed): a longer left tail (common for equity returns).
  • Positive skewness (right‑skewed): a longer right tail.
  • Zero skewness: symmetric distribution (e.g., normal).

Description: Skewness measures the asymmetry of a return distribution. Negative skew means more extreme negative returns (crash risk), while positive skew means more extreme positive returns.

Technical Definition: Sensitivity to Outliers¶

An outlier is an observation that deviates significantly from the bulk of the data. In regression, Cook’s distance measures the influence of a single data point:

$$ D_i = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n} (\hat{y}_j - \hat{y}_{j(i)})^2}{p \cdot \text{MSE}} $$

where $\hat{y}_{j(i)}$ is the predicted value when observation $i$ is omitted, $p$ is the number of predictors, and MSE is the mean squared error. Values > $4/n$ indicate influential outliers.

Description: Outliers disproportionately pull regression coefficients toward extreme values, leading to biased volatility forecasts and mispriced options. Models that ignore outliers produce unstable hedges and underestimate tail risk.

print(f"   Mean increased by {((mean_outlier - mean_normal)/abs(mean_normal))*100:.1f}%")
print(f"   One single outlier distorted both volatility AND expected return estimates!")

Technical Definition: Overfitting¶

Overfitting occurs when a model learns the noise in the training data rather than the underlying signal. Formally, it is detected when the out‑of‑sample prediction error is significantly larger than the in‑sample error:

$$ \text{MSE}_{\text{train}} \ll \text{MSE}_{\text{test}} $$

where MSE is mean squared error. Overfitting is also indicated by large coefficients or a high ratio of model parameters to sample size.

Description: Overfitted volatility models appear accurate on past data but fail to forecast future volatility, leading to poor hedging performance and unexpected losses during option expiration.
